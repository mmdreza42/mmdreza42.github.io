Safety and standards reference. Always follow local code — this is a teaching model, not a construction drawing.

DC arcs do not self-extinguish
Unlike AC, DC has no zero-crossing. A DC arc can sustain across a gap and ignite surrounding material. DC isolators, breakers and MC4 connectors must all be rated for DC at the system voltage.
Anti-islanding
If the grid fails, a grid-following inverter must disconnect within milliseconds so it does not back-feed the network and endanger line workers. Only inverters with an explicit island/backup mode may continue to supply a dedicated backed-up sub-board.
RCD / RCBO vs MCB
An MCB protects against overload and short-circuit. An RCD protects against earth leakage (electrocution). An RCBO combines both. PV arrays typically need a Type B RCD if the inverter can inject DC residual current.
Earthing systems (TN-S, TN-C-S, TT, IT)
TN-S and TN-C-S reference the neutral to earth at the supply. TT uses a local earth electrode at the installation. IT is isolated from earth. PV systems on TT earthing usually require RCD protection.
Lock-out / tag-out sequence
1. Notify users. 2. Shut down the inverter. 3. Open AC isolator. 4. Open DC isolator. 5. Wait for DC-link capacitors to discharge. 6. Verify dead with a tester rated for DC. 7. Apply locks and tags. 8. Begin work.
String over-voltage in cold weather
Voc rises as cell temperature falls. At record-low temperature, the string Voc can exceed the inverter’s maximum DC voltage and destroy it. Always check Voc at the coldest expected cell temperature.
Key standards
IEC 61215 (module design qualification), IEC 61730 (module safety), IEC 62109 (inverter safety), IEC 62446 (commissioning & documentation), IEC 62548 (PV array design), AS/NZS 5033 (Australia/NZ), NEC Article 690 (US).
Protection against DC residual current
Some transformerless inverters inject small DC residual currents that blind a Type A RCD. Use a Type B RCD, or follow the inverter manufacturer’s guidance.
